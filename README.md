## What This Does

A writer sends JSON to `POST /submit` with their text and `creator_id`. The route validates the input, gives the text to the local model signal in `detector.py`, then gives the same text to the stylometry signal in `stylometry.py`. `scoring.py::combine_signals` combines those two scores into one 0–1 AI-likelihood score, and `scoring.py::score_to_label` turns that number into a plain-English label. The decision and both signal scores are written to `logs/audit.jsonl`, and the response returns the content ID, guess, confidence score, reader label, and both signal scores. If the writer disagrees, `POST /appeal` records their reasoning and adds an `under_review` event for the same content ID.

---

## Signals and Scoring

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** `detector.py::model_signal` measures how predictable the submitted text is to a local GPT-2 language model using perplexity. Lower perplexity becomes a higher AI-likelihood score.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** Generated text often follows statistically likely word sequences, so a language model may find it easier to predict than more irregular human writing.

**What it can't see:** Predictability is not authorship. Plain human writing, second-language writing, or writing with common vocabulary can also be highly predictable, while ornate AI text can look less predictable.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** `stylometry.py::style_signal` combines sentence-length spread, vocabulary variety, and expressive punctuation density. Low variation on those measures pushes the score toward AI.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** Generated prose often has more even sentence lengths and more regular vocabulary or punctuation patterns, while human writing can be lumpier and less regular.

**What it can't see:** It cannot understand meaning or authorship. Small edits, typos, sentence breaks, quotations, or punctuation changes can move the score without changing who wrote the text.

### The combining rule

**The rule:** Start with a weighted average. The model signal gets 55% and the style signal gets 45%. If the two signals differ by at least 0.35, the combined score is pulled halfway toward 0.50. That disagreement rule intentionally favors `unsure` over a confident accusation.

**The numbers:** model weight = 0.55, style weight = 0.45, strong-disagreement distance = 0.35, human threshold = 0.30, AI threshold = 0.70.

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:** The service is designed so that when one signal strongly says AI and the other strongly says human, the score moves toward 0.50 instead of trusting either one completely. I record both scores in every response and audit entry so the disagreement is visible.

---

## Label Variants

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0.00 – 0.30 | Our signals suggest this text was probably written by a person. |
| unsure | above 0.30 and below 0.70 | We cannot confidently tell whether this text was written by a person or generated by AI. No accusation should be made from this result. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.70 – 1.00 | Our signals strongly suggest this text may be AI-generated. This is not proof, and the writer can appeal this result. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:** The system is not capable of proving authorship. The label says that clearly and avoids turning uncertainty into an accusation.

## Rate Limiting

**My limits:** 10 submissions per minute and 200 submissions per day for each `creator_id`.

**Why those numbers and not others:** A normal writer should be able to submit or revise a few pieces without being blocked. Ten a minute allows that, while slowing a script that sends hundreds of near-identical submissions to probe the detector. The daily cap limits sustained probing. These are starter limits for a local service, not production-tested thresholds.

**What a caller counts as:** Each nonblank `creator_id` gets its own limit. Requests without a valid creator ID fall back to the requester's IP address. That means a script changing its creator ID can evade the per-creator limit, while unrelated people without IDs on the same IP can share a limit.
